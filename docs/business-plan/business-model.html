Decide · business model

Charge for confidence, not GPU time

Training compute is cheap and provider margins belong to infrastructure vendors. Crux should monetize the high-value work around evidence quality, release confidence, cross-provider decisions, and ongoing specialist governance.

Pricing: hypothesesInitial motion: services-assistedLong-term: recurring control plane
Business-model thesis: OSS earns trust and adoption; fixed-scope reliability engagements create early revenue and learning; a hosted or VPC evidence-and-certification control plane captures recurring value once repeated workflows exist.

The value equation

Annual customer value = inference savings + human review avoided + incidents avoided + latency/revenue gain + premium customization revenue − new serving and operating cost

Crux should be priced against the next best alternative: internal engineering and ML effort, fragmented eval/observability tooling, unreliable releases, and provider lock-in. It should not be priced as a percentage markup on training tokens.

What stays free

Open source forever
  • Core harness primitives and provider adapters.
  • Local observability and developer workflow.
  • Local Evals, automatic evidence reuse, Baselines, and Review.
  • Case inspection and portable export contracts.
  • A complete path to use Crux without a hosted account.
Paid convenience / coordination
  • Team evidence ingestion, retention, review, and lineage.
  • Production monitoring and drift workflows.
  • Cross-provider bake-offs and certification history.
  • Approval, promotion, rollback, audit, and policy controls.
  • Managed orchestration that uses customer-chosen providers.

Preferred value metric

Pricing metric

Active certified Specialists + monitored production decisions

An active Specialist maps to a valuable bounded job; production decisions scale with delivered coverage. Avoid per-seat pricing for a developer platform and avoid training-token pricing that invites comparison with commodity compute.

In the nearer reliability product, “Specialist” can initially mean a protected workflow even before it has custom weights. This keeps the commercial object stable as delivery moves from prompting to routing or fine-tuning.

Early engagement economics

Target duration
2–4 wk

Enough to produce an outcome; short enough to constrain scope.

Founder time
5–10 d

Delivery must leave time for interviews and productization.

Gross margin
Learning

Early margin is secondary; repeated manual work is product discovery.

Every engagement must produce at least two of: cash, reusable product capability, a quantified case study, new qualified introductions, or decisive invalidation. If it produces only bespoke code, decline it.

These scenarios test business shape. They exclude churn, tax, cost, timing, and probability and are not forecasts.

Capital strategy

Remain capital-efficient until there is evidence that money accelerates a working loop. Good reasons to raise later include enterprise security requirements, a repeatable sales motion, or infrastructure needed for a proven cross-provider product. “We can build more features” is not a financing thesis.

Revenue gate

Ask for money before building the cloud

After at most two exceptional free design partners, quote a fixed-scope paid engagement. If qualified teams value the outcome but repeatedly refuse to pay, investigate whether the buyer, pain, proof, or delivery model is wrong.

Founder action

Create a one-page pilot statement of work

Define workflow, evidence access, success metric, deliverables, exclusions, timeline, price, case-study terms, and paid continuation decision. Keep it understandable without legal or procurement support.
