Learn · market evidence

The need is real. The easy layer is crowded.

Companies pay to evaluate, customize, and operate AI. They also report large gains from narrow open models. But infrastructure vendors are rapidly bundling training, evaluation, deployment, and data flywheels. Crux must own a layer they do not naturally own.

Primary sources refreshed 2026-07-12Vendor outcomes labeled
Market conclusion: there is a large and funded market for custom production AI, but “upload a dataset and fine-tune” is already a commodity. The plausible opening is provider-neutral, harness-aware evidence construction and independent certification.

Problems with economic weight

Quality ceiling

Prompting stops improving

A generic model is broadly capable but inconsistent on one company’s rules, tools, schemas, or edge cases.

Unit economics

Frontier calls tax every use

Repeated, narrow workflows pay for general intelligence on every request and often carry unnecessary latency.

Operations

Evidence is fragmented

Logs, user edits, evals, human review, prompts, and training datasets live in different systems with weak lineage.

Deployment

Better weights are not enough

Teams still need held-out proof, canaries, fallbacks, drift detection, rollback, and approval.

Governance

Production data is sensitive

Consent, retention, redaction, export eligibility, and deletion must survive the path from interaction to weights.

Talent

The workflow needs specialists

Most product teams can call a trainer; few can design trustworthy datasets, rewards, and release gates.

The training layer is commoditizing

$0.50

Fireworks list price per 1M LoRA SFT training tokens for models up to 16B, as checked 2026-07-12.

$0.48

Together list price per 1M LoRA SFT training tokens for models up to 16B, with minimum charges and separate hosting economics.

The exact prices will change. The durable conclusion is that training compute alone cannot support Crux’s margin. Fireworks also offers RFT, evaluation, multi-LoRA, and provider-hosted deployment. Together spans inference, fine-tuning, and dedicated capacity. Predibase now sits inside Rubrik. OpenPipe joined CoreWeave and announced that its legacy training and inference product is migrating into Weights & Biases in July 2026.

Positioning recommendation

Do not compete with the trainer

Crux should produce a portable learning and certification package: task contract, governed evidence, splits, preferences or rewards, independent evals, provider job configuration, and promotion decision. Fireworks or Together can train and host it; Crux remains valuable when the provider changes.

Evidence that budgets exist

Evaluation vendors charge recurring platform and usage fees: Braintrust lists a $249/month Pro plan, LangSmith charges by retained traces and collaboration tier, and Arize offers paid cloud plus custom enterprise plans. Infrastructure funding is substantial: Fireworks announced a $250M round at a $4B valuation in October 2025; Together announced $305M at a $3.3B valuation in February 2025. This demonstrates strategic interest, but also the intensity of competition.

Market gate

The category is not the customer

Do not interpret funding rounds as product-market fit for Crux. Continue only when direct interviews reveal repeated pain that buyers cannot cheaply solve inside their existing provider or observability platform.

Founder action

Test three pains, not one pitch

In interviews, separately test: (1) diagnosing harness failures, (2) turning production evidence into reliable evals, and (3) replacing a repeated frontier-model workflow with a certified specialist. Record which pain has urgency, budget, and an existing workaround.
