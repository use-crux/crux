Execute · metrics and risks

Measure pull, not motion

The scorecard must tell the founder whether Crux is becoming useful, retained, and valuable—not whether the repository looks busy. Risks carry invalidation signals so optimism cannot silently rewrite the plan.

Review weeklyDecide monthlyReset quarterly
Measurement rule: an external behavior is stronger than an internal output. A retained workflow beats a star; a paid continuation beats a compliment; a customer-provided dataset beats a roadmap vote.

Monthly decision questions

  1. What did an external user do repeatedly this month?
  2. Which problem appeared in at least three independent conversations?
  3. What did someone pay for—or refuse to pay for?
  4. Which manual step repeated enough to become product?
  5. What assumption became weaker?
  6. Which roadmap item should be deleted?
  7. Is the next constraint product, distribution, trust, or evidence?
Operating recommendation

Maintain one public score and one private truth

Publicly share useful adoption and outcome evidence when honest. Privately track rejected offers, failed activations, inactive users, and support cost. The private ledger protects decisions from launch-day optimism.

Company gate

Reassess after 90 days of real discovery

If ten qualified interviews and at least one serious design-partner attempt produce no repeated urgent problem, revisit the front-door use case and target customer. Do not automatically abandon the technical asset or automatically continue the specialization thesis.

Founder action

Create the scorecard now

Start with zeros. Review it weekly. The discomfort of an empty external column is the intended counterweight to effortless implementation progress.
