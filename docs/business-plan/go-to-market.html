Decide · go to market

Sell the learning before the platform

A solo founder with no capital cannot win through advertising, enterprise procurement, or content volume. Crux should combine OSS trust, direct problem discovery, a hands-on reliability offer, and public proof—then automate only what repeats.

Motion: founder-ledBudget: near zeroPrimary currency: evidence
GTM thesis: design partnerships are not consulting as a detour. They are paid product research that creates users, customer language, datasets, proof, and the specification for a repeatable commercial workflow.

The first offer

Offer · Reliability Sprint

Make one production AI workflow explainable, testable, and cheaper to change

Two to four weeks. Crux instruments one bounded workflow, reproduces representative failures, establishes a baseline, tests one meaningful change, and delivers an evidence report plus prioritized next steps. It may conclude that training is unnecessary.

Customer provides
  • Technical owner and domain reviewer.
  • One workflow, representative cases, and access to sanitized evidence.
  • A measurable success criterion.
  • Two review sessions and permission to discuss anonymized learnings.
Crux delivers
  • Instrumented authored/runtime/Eval view.
  • Failure taxonomy and root-cause examples.
  • Golden Cases, scorers, Baseline, and release Gate.
  • Model/harness comparison with cost and latency.
  • Recommendation: keep, route, retrieve, or specialize.

The content strategy is evidence

Do not maintain a generic “AI tips” calendar. Every public artifact should arise from a real product question:

  • Why did this turn include the wrong context?
  • How do we protect a model migration with harness baselines?
  • When does a small fine-tuned model beat routing or few-shot examples?
  • How do user edits become preference data without leaking personal memory?
  • What does a trustworthy RL reward require?

Each piece should contain runnable code, a decision framework, measured results, and limitations. This is distribution that also improves the product.

What not to do

  • Do not launch another broad homepage rewrite without testing it on unfamiliar developers.
  • Do not chase repository stars as the primary goal.
  • Do not build a generic hosted observability dashboard before a paid workflow requires it.
  • Do not promise continuous RL, enterprise governance, or one-model-per-customer as shipped.
  • Do not accept an engagement whose core deliverable is bespoke application development.
GTM gate

No platform build before repeated manual pain

A commercial feature enters the roadmap only after the founder performs the same high-value step for at least three teams and can describe its input, output, buyer, frequency, and willingness to pay.
