Learn · current position

Built ahead of adoption

Crux is not an idea-stage product. It is a technically ambitious toolkit with a source-linked graph, runtime evidence, and an evaluation system. Commercially, however, it is still at zero: the category, wedge, onboarding path, and willingness to pay remain unproven.

Evidence: repositoryCommercial confidence: low
The core mismatch: Crux has solved many difficult implementation problems before establishing the one narrow problem that pulls strangers through installation, activation, repeated use, and payment.

What exists today

Composition

One way into the turn

Typed prompts, contexts, memory, retrieval, tools, safety, routing, orchestration, and Quality compose around the user’s SDK rather than replacing it.

Evidence

One graph

Authored definitions, runtime spans, decision evidence, and quality records can be joined instead of living in separate dashboards.

Proof

One quality system

Cases, variants, trials, scorers, gates, cassettes, and promoted baselines create a credible foundation for test-driven harness work.

Constraints are strategic, not computational

Founder constraints
  • One developer, no sales or marketing function.
  • No capital for paid distribution or long enterprise procurement cycles.
  • A broad repository that can absorb unlimited implementation time.
  • Unknown customer language and no case-study proof.
Useful freedoms
  • Agentic development makes product iteration unusually fast.
  • OSS permits trust, inspection, contribution, and bottom-up discovery.
  • No legacy customers or revenue commitments restrict positioning.
  • Provider neutrality allows partnerships rather than infrastructure competition.

The current adoption problem

The public surface offers many honest entry points—prompts, context, memory, retrieval, safety, routing, evals, observability—but breadth makes the first reason to care less obvious. A developer must understand “harness engineering,” choose among many packages, and imagine the payoff before experiencing it.

Awareness × clear pain × fast first value × repeated workflow = adoption

Crux currently controls mostly the last two terms—and has not yet made either undeniable.
Recommendation

Narrow the front door, not the architecture

Keep the modular platform internally, but lead externally with one job: find why a production AI turn failed, fix the harness, and prove the regression cannot return. The specialization horizon should make the story more valuable, not make the first installation more complex.

Unfair advantage—if completed

Observability vendors see what ran. Training providers see the dataset and optimization job. Crux can potentially see the authored harness, resolved turn, runtime outcome, quality verdict, and source definition together. That creates an upstream evidence layer with three defensible properties:

  1. Intent: what the developer authored and expected.
  2. Reality: what the model actually received and did.
  3. Proof: which cases, baselines, and decisions establish acceptable behavior.

This advantage remains theoretical until external users experience it and retain the workflow.

Decision gate

Stop expanding breadth without activation evidence

A new primitive should not outrank onboarding, the flagship proof, or a design-partner requirement unless it closes a clear blocker for an active workflow.

Founder action

Create an activation baseline

Ask three developers unfamiliar with Crux to install it and reach one useful explained turn plus one passing Quality case. Record where they stop, how long it takes, and which concepts require explanation. That becomes the first product funnel.
